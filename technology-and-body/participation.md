# Technology and embodied participation

[Home](../README.md)

A digital interaction takes place somewhere: a noisy room, a shared device, a chair that may not fit, a conversation that runs longer than intended. Those conditions can be worth examining without claiming that every difficulty is bodily or that a brief practice fixes structural problems.

Before a long interaction, name its purpose and a review point. During it, ask whether the format still supports participation. Afterward, preserve the decisions you need and choose how to transition. These are educational process suggestions, not claims about improved cognition.

An adaptive system can change the pace and direction of an exchange. A person can value that responsiveness and still need boundaries around time, data, delegated action, or continuity. [Interaction Literacy's delegation module](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/curriculum/02-capability-authority-delegation.md) examines those boundaries. A bodily check-in cannot enforce a permission or revoke access; actual communication and system controls remain necessary.

## Reflective exercise

Choose a fictional or low-stakes task. Map purpose, interface, physical setting, available alternatives, and stopping conditions. Which constraint can the individual change? Which belongs to the institution or provider? Propose one change in each category and explain how you would assess whether it helped. Keep experience reports separate from physiological explanations.
