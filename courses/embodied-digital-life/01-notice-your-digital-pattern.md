# Unit 1 — Notice your digital pattern

[Course](README.md) · [Next: Attention and the urge to check](02-attention-and-the-urge-to-check.md)

**Time:** about 25 minutes, with flexible pauses. **You need:** this text and any accessible way to think or make private notes. No account or personal disclosure is needed.

**Learning outcome:** describe a digital situation, distinguish observation from explanation, and select a change worth considering.

## 1. Start with a situation — 3 minutes

**Fictional case:** Mira opens a shared document to prepare three questions for a community workshop. They intend to work for twenty minutes. A message arrives, followed by a suggested article and another comment on the document. Forty minutes later, Mira has read several things but written one question. The room has become noisy. They say, “Technology has ruined my concentration.”

Before interpreting the case, identify three details you could point to in its description. Then name one thing the description does not tell you. You may read the case aloud, ask someone to read it, or work silently.

An observation is something reported or recorded, such as “one question was written.” An interpretation explains an observation, such as “the message made it harder to return.” Interpretations may be reasonable while remaining uncertain. A broad claim about a damaged brain requires evidence that this case does not provide.

## 2. Learn what a study can tell us — 5 minutes

Researchers sometimes examine whether changing a habit changes a measured outcome. In a randomized trial, assignment to different conditions helps test the effect of the assigned intervention. It does not make every detail of the intervention necessary for everyone, or identify every mechanism behind a result.

Pieh and colleagues studied 111 healthy students, averaging about 23 years old, who used smartphones at least three hours a day. Those assigned to reduce use to two hours or less daily for three weeks showed small-to-medium improvements in several self-reported outcomes, including stress, well-being, depressive symptoms, and sleep-related symptoms, compared with the control group. The study was unblinded, and use rose again after the intervention. It did not measure whether brains had been damaged or repaired. [E4: study and limits](../../research-and-reading/digital-life-evidence.md#e4-changing-smartphone-use).

This gives a reason to investigate patterns of use. It does not establish two hours as a universal healthy limit, or prove that reducing Mira's access is the right answer. Mira's task, surroundings, interruptions, and access needs all deserve attention.

## 3. Map the pattern — 6 minutes

Complete these six prompts for Mira. You may substitute an ordinary personal example, but do not choose a crisis or record private messages, health information, or other people's details.

| Prompt | Case information or question |
| --- | --- |
| Intended task | What were they trying to finish? |
| Digital conditions | Which features or events competed with it? |
| Physical and social setting | What else about the situation might matter? |
| Observed outcome | What happened, without assigning a cause? |
| Possible explanations | Name two explanations; more than one may contribute. |
| Available change | What could Mira change, and what would require someone else's cooperation? |

For example, Mira can mark a place to resume after a message. A team expectation that every comment receives an immediate reply would require a shared conversation. These are different kinds of change. An individual practice cannot create authority to revise a shared rule.

## 4. Try an optional arrival — 3 minutes

Use [Practice A: Arrive with a purpose](practices.md#a-arrive-with-a-purpose), or simply complete this sentence for Mira: “I am here to ____; I will review whether to continue after ____.” Add a boundary about what the activity does not authorize.

Movement, breath attention, inward attention, and closing the screen are unnecessary. Keep assistive technology active. You may take an ordinary break instead and return to the fictional case. The learning task is to name a purpose and a review point; no particular bodily response is required.

The existing [arriving practice](../../practices/01-arriving.md) offers a longer version for an AI interaction. Like this course's version, it is an educational invitation, not an evaluated intervention.

## 5. Design one next use — 5 minutes

Choose a modest change for the next workshop-planning session. Examples include opening the required document directly, giving non-urgent messages a later review point, finding an accessible quieter location if available, or agreeing a twenty-minute drafting period with collaborators. Preserve the channels needed for communication and access.

Name a simple observation that would tell Mira whether the change is workable: whether the three questions were drafted, whether returning after a message was straightforward, or whether the arrangement created a new barrier. Do not ask Mira to achieve calm or a lower screen-time total as a condition of success.

Use [Workbook section 1](workbook.md#1-situation-map) if a reusable format helps.

## 6. Check your response — 3 minutes

**Question:** A learner says, “Mira felt more settled after the arrival practice, so we know it repaired their attention.” How would you revise that statement?

**Response guidance:** A careful answer might say, “Mira reported feeling more settled. We do not yet know whether their task performance changed or why. The practice may be worth keeping if they find it useful, while still examining the interruptions and environment.” The proposed biological repair is unsupported. Equally, Mira could feel no different and still benefit from a clearer task plan.

**Exit response:** name one observation, one uncertain explanation, and one practical change. If your response distinguishes those three things and preserves choice, it meets this unit's learning goal.

**Next use:** before another low-stakes session, name its purpose and one review point. You may use the fictional case again instead of tracking your own activity.
