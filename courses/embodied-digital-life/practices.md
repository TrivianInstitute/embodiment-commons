# Optional practice cards and read-aloud scripts

[Course](README.md) · Original educational invitations · Unpiloted

These cards make a pause or transition available within a lesson. They are not the interventions used in the studies cited by the course. No physiological, clinical, or learning benefit is promised. A useful, neutral, uncomfortable, or untried response is valid information about the invitation.

**Choice at the start:** follow a card, use its task-only alternative, use a familiar accessible practice, or take an ordinary break. Keep necessary assistive and communication technology active. No one needs to explain a choice. A facilitator may read the words below and leave flexible space; they must not count, pace, or evaluate another person's breathing or movement.

Do not use these cards while driving, crossing traffic, or performing work requiring continuous attention. Movement stays within a familiar, comfortable range. Stop any activity that becomes painful, dizzying, breathless, or otherwise uncomfortable. There is no requirement to return to it.

## A. Arrive with a purpose

**Time:** 1–3 minutes. **Fits:** before a non-urgent digital task. **Learning purpose:** distinguish the intended task from the activity available on the screen.

### Read-aloud or self-guided script

“Mark your place, or leave the next activity unopened for a moment. Keep anything you need for access available.

“Choose a comfortable external feature, a familiar point of support, or the words of the task itself. You do not need to examine your body. There is no particular feeling to find.

“Complete this sentence in any private form: I am here to ____.

“Choose a point when you will review whether to continue: after the first response, after this page, or another point that fits. Then name something this task does not authorize, such as sending a message or sharing another person's information.

“Begin, change the conditions, or defer. Keep the purpose available if that helps.”

**Task-only alternative:** read the fictional task in the lesson and give it a purpose, boundary, and review point. No sensory attention or movement is involved.

**Private reflection:** Did a purpose or boundary become clearer? What remains unchanged? Do not infer improved judgment from the feeling of being prepared.

**Related existing card:** [Arriving before an AI interaction](../../practices/01-arriving.md).

## B. Ordinary breath or an external point

**Time:** 1–3 minutes, chosen by the participant. **Fits:** at a recoverable point in a non-urgent exchange. **Learning purpose:** make room to choose a next action while separating experience from interpretation.

### Read-aloud or self-guided script

“Save your place. You may stop looking at the current content while keeping needed technology active.

“Choose one option: notice your ordinary breathing without changing it; notice a comfortable external feature; or simply read the next decision question. You may change options or finish at any time.

“If you chose breathing, let it continue in its usual way. There is no need to slow it, deepen it, count it, hold it, or match anybody else's rhythm. If attention to breathing is unwelcome, leave it alone.

“If you chose an external feature, notice only what is comfortable: perhaps a sound, a familiar object, or a detail of your surroundings. You need not search or scan.

“Now return to the question: What needs to happen next, and what can wait? A feeling that arose during the pause is not an answer to a factual question. Choose a next step on the basis of the task, available evidence, and your authority to act.”

**Task-only alternative:** write or consider “continue, seek information, change a condition, or stop,” and choose one for the fictional example. An ordinary break is equally acceptable.

**Private reflection:** Was this version useful, neutral, uncomfortable, or not tried? Would a different option be preferable next time? No one has to report a bodily sensation.

**Related existing cards:** [Decision pause](../../practices/03-decision-pause.md) and [Noticing uncertainty](../../practices/06-uncertainty.md).

## C. A small seated movement or a change in the task

**Time:** 2–4 minutes, with any amount of that time used for rest. **Fits:** between digital tasks. **Learning purpose:** notice that the organization of a work session can include movement and access choices.

### Read-aloud or self-guided script

“Save the current work. Choose to remain as you are, make a familiar small movement, or work with the task-only alternative. Nobody will touch or correct your position.

“If movement is comfortable and appropriate for you, choose one possibility: a small adjustment in your seated position, an easy movement of your hands or shoulders, or a familiar change between resting against your support and sitting a little more upright. Keep the movement small and within a range you already know is comfortable. You do not need to coordinate it with breathing.

“You can repeat, change, or stop without reaching a target. There is no stretch depth, repetition count, ideal posture, or feeling of release to achieve. If a movement feels uncomfortable, stop it.

“Notice a practical choice for the next task: keep the current arrangement, adjust an object within safe reach, request another format, or take a longer break. If you wish, name one concrete starting action before returning.”

**Task-only alternative:** remain still and decide where a break or format change belongs in the fictional schedule. You may also change attention without moving. These are equivalent ways to complete the learning task, without a claim that they have the same physiological effects as movement.

**Private reflection:** Which part of the arrangement could be made easier? Was the movement invitation accessible? Leaving the arrangement unchanged is an option.

**Yoga context:** attentive, comfortable postural exploration has connections with some contemporary gentle yoga teaching. This original invitation is not a prescribed asana sequence or a pain-treatment program.

**Related existing card:** [Movement transition](../../practices/05-movement-transition.md).

## D. External attention and visual choice

**Time:** 1–3 minutes. **Fits:** after sustained focus on a page or screen. **Learning purpose:** widen awareness of the immediate setting and choose whether to change it.

### Read-aloud or self-guided script

“Mark your place. Keep any needed display or assistive device active.

“Choose an available, comfortable external channel. You might notice a familiar sound, an object, a point of contact with a support, or a mental map of the room. One is enough.

“If you wish to use vision, you may look away from the task toward a comfortable farther-away object. Do not stare, strain, seek bright light, or force your eyes into a position. Blinking can stay ordinary. You may choose another channel instead.

“Identify one practical feature: an object is hard to reach, the text is small, the sound is distracting, or the current setup works. Choose an adjustment you can make, a request you can offer, or no change.

“Return to the task, take an ordinary break, or finish. This observation does not establish that a setting is safe or suitable for everyone.”

**Task-only alternative:** review the lesson's fictional workstation and choose one access improvement. No seeing, hearing, touch, or body scanning is required.

**Private reflection:** What did you notice about the setting? What would require asking someone else rather than assuming their needs?

**Related existing cards:** [Sensory practice](../../practices/02-sensory-reset.md) and [Environmental orientation](../../practices/07-environmental-orientation.md). The earlier card's word “reset” names a transition; it does not claim a biological reset.

## E. Supported rest and a clear ending

**Time:** 2–5 minutes, or a brief ordinary pause. **Fits:** the end of a session or a transition toward rest. **Learning purpose:** end an activity without leaving the next action undefined.

### Read-aloud or self-guided script

“Choose a supported position that already works for you. You may stay seated, remain in your existing position, or use another familiar arrangement. Moving to the floor, closing the eyes, or lying down is not required.

“Write, say privately, or think through what is finished and what remains. Save only the task information you need. Keep private material out of shared notes.

“Choose a place to resume: tomorrow's first question, the next paragraph, or a named decision. You do not need to solve it now.

“Let this interval contain no additional task if that is available to you. You may keep your eyes open and your breathing ordinary. You may also skip the interval and move directly to the next part of your day.

“When you choose, end the pause. Follow the transition you planned. Rest does not have to produce a report or a particular state.”

**Task-only alternative:** create an ending note and a resuming point for the fictional task. If stopping is not possible now, identify the next available opportunity or a schedule change to request.

**Private reflection:** Was the ending practical? What kept the task open? A short rest does not replace sleep or resolve an inaccessible workload.

**Related existing card:** [Collaboration check-in](../../practices/04-collaboration-check-in.md).

## A facilitator's closing question

“Which option would you keep, adapt, or omit next time?” Responses may concern the instructions alone. A pass is complete. Discuss access, timing, and choice; do not request personal symptoms or explanations of another person's nervous system.
