# Unit 5 — Transitions into rest

[Previous: Movement and visual comfort](04-movement-and-visual-comfort.md) · [Course](README.md) · [Next: Return to choice](06-return-to-choice.md)

**Time:** about 25 minutes. **You need:** this text and an accessible way to plan; no sleep tracking or personal health information is requested.

**Learning outcome:** distinguish a studied light effect from other possible reasons a digital activity runs into rest time, and design a practical ending that preserves needed access.

## 1. Find what keeps the activity open — 3 minutes

**Fictional case:** Noor plans to finish preparing a short workshop description before their usual rest period. An AI offers several promising alternatives and invites another round. Noor keeps refining the description. When they finally stop, they have not recorded which version they chose or where to resume. They reopen the conversation to check. Their phone also provides an important communication channel they intend to keep available.

List the unfinished parts of the task. What would allow Noor to stop without losing the work? Treat their useful communication channel as a design requirement.

## 2. Distinguish light, time, and content — 5 minutes

Chang and colleagues compared reading on a light-emitting electronic device with reading a printed book in twelve healthy adults. Participants read for four hours before bedtime over five evenings in each condition. The device condition delayed circadian timing, suppressed evening melatonin, lengthened sleep onset, and reduced next-morning alertness. This was a small, controlled crossover experiment with a particular exposure schedule. It does not predict an exact effect from a few minutes on every device. [E3: evening-reading study](../../research-and-reading/digital-life-evidence.md#e3-evening-light-and-sleep).

For Noor's case, separate three questions:

- **Light:** what light exposure occurs near the intended sleep period? The study above bears on this pathway within its tested conditions.
- **Time:** is work continuing into time Noor had allocated to sleep or another form of rest? This can be seen in the schedule without assuming a biological mechanism.
- **Content and unfinished tasks:** what makes Noor want to reopen the conversation? The case suggests possibilities, but does not establish which caused a sleep problem.

Do not collapse these into one claim that all screen use has the same effect. This lesson does not diagnose insomnia or prescribe a treatment. A brightness change alone would also leave Noor's unfinished decision and repeated reopening unresolved.

## 3. Build an ending and a resuming point — 6 minutes

Draft a short ending note for Noor. It should include:

1. The chosen version or the decision still open.
2. The next concrete step, limited to one sentence.
3. A point when work can resume.
4. The communications or assistive functions that remain available.

For example: “Use version B as the working draft. At the next session, check the room-access details before sharing. Review after breakfast; keep the family contact channel active.” The timing can be adapted to a person's schedule, including work or caregiving at night. The cited evening-light study does not establish a single routine for every schedule.

Choose one additional environmental change to consider: close the nonessential thread, move the saved draft out of a feed, reduce avoidable bright light where practical, or agree a later deadline with collaborators. These are proposed design choices. Do not promise that any one of them will produce a certain sleep outcome.

Use [Workbook section 5](workbook.md#5-ending-and-resuming) to keep the plan brief.

## 4. Try supported rest or the ending note alone — 3 minutes

Use [Practice E: Supported rest and a clear ending](practices.md#e-supported-rest-and-a-clear-ending). A familiar supported position is enough; you may remain where you are. Eyes may stay open. Breathing remains ordinary. Moving to the floor, lying down, internal attention, or a guided body scan is unnecessary.

Alternatively, finish Noor's ending note and take an ordinary break. You can keep an accessible display or communication aid active. No one should judge participation by visible relaxation or ask you to report whether the practice made you sleepy.

This rest invitation is an original course activity, not the intervention in the evening-reading study. It does not reverse light exposure or substitute for sleep.

## 5. Make the transition realistic — 5 minutes

Imagine Noor has only a short interval before another responsibility. Which part of the plan is still possible? A useful answer may be as small as recording the next step and closing the nonessential thread. It may also identify a structural problem: an impossible deadline, a schedule with no protected rest, or expectations of continuous availability.

Name which condition Noor can change and which needs a request or agreement. Do not make a relaxation practice the price of tolerating an unreasonable schedule. If no personal adjustment is practical, stating what needs to change is still meaningful learning.

Choose an observation for the next use: whether the task was reopened, whether the next step was easy to find, or whether the planned ending was feasible. This course does not require a sleep diary, wearable data, symptom scale, or disclosure of why rest is difficult. Persistent or concerning sleep difficulties deserve appropriate professional attention; a course reflection cannot assess them.

## 6. Check your response — 3 minutes

**Question:** “The reading experiment proves that a three-minute rest practice cancels the effect of an evening screen.” What does the study actually support, and what could Noor reasonably try?

**Response guidance:** The experiment compared particular reading exposures; it did not test this rest practice or the cancellation claim. Noor can make the task easier to end, consider avoidable light exposure, preserve essential access, and see whether the chosen routine is workable. A failure to feel relaxed does not invalidate the ending note or establish that Noor tried incorrectly.

**Exit response:** identify one distinction among light, time, and content; give a resuming point; and state one access function you would protect.

**Next use:** write the next step before leaving a digital task. Use a rest invitation only if it fits; the ending note itself can be the completed activity.
