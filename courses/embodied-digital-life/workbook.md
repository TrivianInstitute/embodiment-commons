# Embodied Digital Life — Learner workbook

[Course](README.md) · [Practice cards](practices.md) · [Evidence ledger](../../research-and-reading/digital-life-evidence.md)

Use this text privately on paper, in an accessible note, through speech, drawing, or thought. A facilitator can read each prompt aloud. You may answer every question using the course's fictional cases; personal reflection is optional. Suggested answers illustrate the reasoning, not a required personal response.

Do not put health information, private messages, identifiable quotations, or another person's behavior in a shared workbook. Keep your notes outside public GitHub issues and repositories. If a learning response is requested, submit a fictional analysis or a general process change. You do not need to prove participation with device logs or disclose why you need an accommodation.

## 1. Situation map

Use after [Unit 1](01-notice-your-digital-pattern.md).

**Case reminder:** Mira intended to write three workshop questions in twenty minutes. Messages and recommendations drew them elsewhere. After forty minutes they had one question; the room was also noisier.

Complete these prompts:

1. The intended task was ____.
2. The observed digital events were ____.
3. Other conditions in the setting were ____.
4. An observation I can point to is ____.
5. One possible explanation is ____; another is ____.
6. A change within the person's control is ____.
7. A change requiring a shared agreement or request is ____.
8. An observation that would help assess the change is ____.

**Worked example:** “Mira wrote one question in forty minutes” is an observation. “Mira cannot concentrate anymore” is a broader interpretation. A resuming note could be tested; expectations about immediate replies may need a team agreement.

**Check:** have you described what happened without claiming a diagnosis, permanent change, or biological repair? Did you consider a condition beyond individual willpower?

## 2. Interruption plan

Use after [Unit 2](02-attention-and-the-urge-to-check.md).

**Case reminder:** a like notification competes with Dev's venue comparison. The same device supplies a screen reader and a needed reminder.

Complete the sequence:

- The invitation to change tasks is ____.
- The current question to return to is ____.
- Two available responses are ____ and ____.
- The chosen review point is ____.
- A setting or workflow change to consider is ____.
- Access functions that must remain available are ____.
- I would revise the plan if ____.

**Evidence question:** which is a better match for the studies: “A notification can interfere with a demanding task in studied conditions” or “Likes permanently damage everyone's dopamine”? Explain the difference in one sentence.

**Response guidance:** the first stays close to the observed task effect. The second adds an unmeasured chemical-damage claim and generalizes to everyone. A suitable plan may separate notification categories or use a resuming cue, rather than remove necessary access.

## 3. Experience, interpretation, and decision

Use after [Unit 3](03-emotional-momentum-and-breath.md).

**Case reminder:** an AI interprets a sharp comment as bad faith. Sam feels urgency and considers sending a forceful reply.

Write three separate entries:

| Entry | Your response |
| --- | --- |
| Observation or reported experience | What does the case actually say? |
| Interpretation and an alternative | What explanation has been added? What else might explain the comment? |
| Decision requirements | What evidence, context, or authorization matters before action? |

Choose a practical boundary: ____. Choose an optional pause, task-only analysis, or ordinary break: ____. A practice I would not require of someone else is ____.

**Evidence question:** what differs between the researched breathing interventions and this course's ordinary, unpaced breath-awareness option? Name at least one difference in the activity, duration, or outcome.

**Response guidance:** a study of one technique practiced repeatedly does not guarantee the effect of this brief invitation. “I feel calmer” and “I verified the disputed date” are different kinds of information. The verified date can justify a correction; calmness alone cannot establish the commenter's intention or the accuracy of the AI response.

## 4. An accessible session redesign

Use after [Unit 4](04-movement-and-visual-comfort.md).

**Case reminder:** Lee attends a ninety-minute workshop with small instructions, busy chat, no break, and a standing invitation that would interrupt access.

Design four parts of a better arrangement:

1. **Content:** how will the essential instructions remain available?
2. **Schedule:** where can a break occur without new required content being introduced?
3. **Choice:** what can someone do if they remain seated or still, avoid inward attention, or keep a screen active?
4. **Review:** what will the host ask about the design without requesting health information?

Write a replacement invitation in two sentences. For example: “We have three minutes before the next section, and the instructions are available in the shared text. You can move in a familiar comfortable way, remain as you are, use another attention option, or take an ordinary break while keeping any access technology active.”

**Check:** the alternative should preserve the learning opportunity. It does not need to reproduce a movement's physical effects. Do not assess success through standing, camera use, stillness, or reports of relaxation.

## 5. Ending and resuming

Use after [Unit 5](05-transitions-into-rest.md).

**Case reminder:** Noor keeps refining an AI-assisted draft and reopens the thread because the next step is undefined. A useful communication channel must stay available.

Create an ending note:

- What is finished or selected? ____
- What remains open? ____
- What is the single next step? ____
- When or at what task boundary can work resume? ____
- What communication or assistive functions remain available? ____

Then identify one condition you would consider changing: the light exposure, the time allocated, the unfinished work, or a shared expectation. State what the course's evening-reading study can support and what it does not test.

**Response guidance:** a good note prevents the open task from becoming an undefined invitation to continue. The light study does not establish that this course's rest practice cancels screen exposure or treats a sleep problem. If the schedule itself is unreasonable, name the change that needs to be requested.

## 6. A small reversible experiment

Use with [Unit 6](06-return-to-choice.md). Planning alone completes this exercise. An optional later trial can use a few ordinary sessions over a week. Do not create excessive use, sleep loss, discomfort, or missed reminders for the sake of comparison.

### Plan

**My practical question:** ____.

**The ordinary task, or fictional case, I will use:** ____.

**What usually happens, or what I will observe in up to two ordinary sessions:** ____.

**One change to try:** ____.

**Optional practice or alternative, if included:** ____.

**One modest observation:** ____.

**Access and communications that stay available:** ____.

**Reasons to stop, adapt, or abandon the change:** ____.

**When I will review whether to keep it:** ____.

If your change combines an environmental adjustment and a practice, label it as a package. Your observations will not tell you which element caused a difference. If you prefer to compare a single change, keep the rest of the arrangement similar where practical; everyday differences still limit causal conclusions.

### Optional private observation record

Record as little as is useful. Words such as “workable,” “neutral,” “burdensome,” or “not tried” can be enough. No biological readings or symptom scores are needed.

| Session | Arrangement or choice | Practical observation | Other difference or next choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example | One resuming sentence | Found the next step easily | The task was also shorter |
| First observation | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Later observation | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Review | Keep / adapt / stop / insufficient information | Reason: ____ | Remaining uncertainty: ____ |

### Interpret without overclaiming

Complete four statements:

1. “What I observed was ____.”
2. “A possible explanation is ____, but another is ____.”
3. “The practical choice I will make is ____.”
4. “This record does not establish ____.”

**Worked example:** “I located the next paragraph more readily when I left a resuming note. The note may help, although those sessions involved easier material. I will keep using it because it is low effort and currently useful. This does not establish a treatment effect or a change in my brain.”

## Final transfer response

Use Jules's case in [Unit 6](06-return-to-choice.md). In a short paragraph, identify the task and its authority boundary; qualify the dopamine claim; protect access; choose an environmental change and an optional practice or alternative; and name a useful observation together with its limits.

### Self-check and response guidance

| Check | Ready when your response does this | Revise if it does this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence | Names a specific claim and what the source can support | Treats a citation, a feeling, or confident wording as sufficient proof |
| Design | Changes an actual condition or explains what must be requested | Requires an individual to tolerate an inaccessible arrangement |
| Choice and access | Protects needed technology and allows an equivalent learning alternative | Requires breath pacing, movement, silence, cameras, or health disclosure |
| Evaluation | Uses a modest observation and names uncertainty | Claims a cure, neural repair, or failure based on one response |

Reasoned alternatives can meet every check. You are assessing your explanation, not grading your body or another person's participation.

**Closing reflection:** “One capability I will carry into my next digital interaction is ____.” There is nothing to upload. Keep, adapt, or discard your private notes as you choose.
