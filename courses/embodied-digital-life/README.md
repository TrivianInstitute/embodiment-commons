# Embodied Digital Life: Attention, Breath, Movement, and Rest

[Embodiment Commons](../../README.md) · Six units, approximately 20–30 minutes each · Adult general education

**Status: v0.1 draft / formative.** This course has not undergone human practitioner review, a formal accessibility audit, or learner piloting. Its activities are original educational invitations; their effectiveness has not been established.

Digital participation happens through a person, in a place, at a particular time. A conversation can run past the point when it is useful. Notifications can compete with the task you meant to finish. A screen can provide essential access while the surrounding schedule leaves little room for movement or rest. This course helps you examine those conditions and make choices you can explain.

You will study bounded findings about attention, social feedback, evening light, physical comfort, and breathing research. You will then distinguish those findings from your own observations and from the optional practices offered here. The aim is deliberate participation: knowing what you are doing, noticing when the conditions change, and choosing how to continue.

## What you will be able to do

By the end, you should be able to:

- Describe a digital situation using observations rather than an assumed diagnosis or brain mechanism.
- Explain one research finding together with its population, comparison, and limits.
- Change a setting, expectation, or schedule and select an optional embodied practice that fits your circumstances.
- Keep bodily feeling separate from evidence about an AI answer or a consequential decision.
- Evaluate a small personal experiment without treating a pleasant experience as proof of treatment or a neutral experience as failure.

There is no requirement to become calm, sit still, reduce all screen use, or disclose personal information. You may complete every learning task using the fictional examples.

## Begin with what is available

No AI account, social-media account, app, wearable, exercise equipment, or internet connection during the lessons is needed. The text can be printed, read aloud, or used with assistive technology. Paper, an accessible note, private speech, drawing, or thinking without recording all work. Suggested times are flexible; take longer or divide a unit into shorter visits.

Every unit includes a digital situation, a short evidence lesson, an environmental choice, an optional practice, and a response you can check. Read the [practice choices](practices.md) before trying one. Movement, breath attention, internal attention, eye closure, and touch are never required. Keep communication aids, reminders, screen readers, navigation, and other necessary technology active. Take an ordinary break whenever that is more useful.

The course is designed for adults. Educators adapting it for younger learners should select age-appropriate examples, follow their setting's participation requirements, and preserve meaningful alternatives. A study of adolescents does not automatically describe adults, and adult intervention findings do not establish what children need.

## Embodiment and yoga in this course

Here, embodied participation includes movement, fatigue, sensory access, bodily experience, and the conditions of the place where learning happens. A person's experience matters without being treated as a complete explanation of its cause.

Yoga is rooted in Indian philosophical and practice traditions and encompasses more than postures or breathing exercises. Contemporary yoga programs vary substantially, including in the studies that investigate them. Some invitations here are informed by familiar gentle postural attention; they are original Commons learning activities rather than a reproduction of a traditional sequence or a tested yoga treatment. See [NCCIH's overview and evidence limits](../../research-and-reading/digital-life-evidence.md#yoga-and-the-scope-of-the-course). This course provides no practitioner qualification or assisted-stretch instruction. The separate Ahava Way certification curriculum is outside its scope.

## How to check your learning

At the end of each unit, compare your answer with the response guidance. A strong answer connects a concrete observation to a proportionate choice, names an uncertainty, and preserves another person's agency. Reasoned disagreement is welcome.

In the final unit you will analyze a new fictional situation and design a small, reversible experiment. You may use an imagined example for both. Assessment concerns the explanation and the design, not someone's posture, emotional state, practice participation, or private health history. A personally useful change is worth noticing; this course does not turn that experience into a medical finding.

## Use with others

Facilitators should read the [existing facilitation guidance](../../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) and [accessibility guidance](../../accessibility/README.md). Offer practice choices before asking for participation. Do not pace a group's breathing, touch or reposition learners, require cameras, collect personal journals, or interpret someone's appearance as evidence of consent or attention.

The [Educator Commons 60-minute session](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/educator-commons/blob/main/sessions/embodied-digital-life-60-min.md) supports a shorter introduction. The [shared learning route](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/educator-commons/blob/main/pathways/ai-literacy-and-embodied-digital-life.md) connects this course with AI literacy, and the [shared transfer activity](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/educator-commons/blob/main/assessment/commons-transfer.md) checks how the capabilities travel to another situation.
