# Digital life — Evidence, applications, and limits

[Research and reading](README.md) · [Embodied Digital Life course](../courses/embodied-digital-life/README.md)

**Prepared:** 2026-10-11. **Status:** formative educational source review; not a systematic review, clinical guideline, or validation of the course. These sources were selected to support bounded teaching claims and to make important limitations visible. The course and its practice cards have not been practitioner reviewed or piloted.

## How to read this ledger

An empirical finding concerns a defined population, intervention or exposure, comparison, and outcome. An educational application is a proposed way to help someone understand or respond to a situation. They are different claims. Our ordinary-breath, movement, sensory-attention, and rest invitations are not automatically equivalent to the interventions below.

An association does not by itself establish causation. A randomized trial can support a causal inference about assignment to its tested intervention while leaving mechanisms, generalization, and long-term effects unresolved. Guidance from a professional organization is not itself a randomized experiment. Personal experience may inform a practical choice without proving a general effect.

External sources retain their own rights and do not endorse the Commons. The descriptions below are original summaries; no source protocol or copyrighted curriculum is reproduced.

## E1. Notifications and immediate attention

**Source:** Stothart, C., Mitchum, A., & Yehnert, C. (2015). *The attentional cost of receiving a cell phone notification.* Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26121498/) · [Author's study page and manuscript link](https://carystothart.com/publications/attentional-cost-cell-phone-notification/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000100).

**Finding and scope:** in a university-undergraduate experiment (212 participants; 166 in the main analysis), call/text-notification groups showed larger increases in task errors than a no-notification group. The text-message result was less conclusive because groups differed at baseline. This concerns an immediate task effect, not permanent attention loss or a diagnosis.

**Application:** [Unit 2](../courses/embodied-digital-life/02-attention-and-the-urge-to-check.md) asks learners to plan interruptions while preserving access. The proposed pause is not a tested restoration protocol.

## E2. Social feedback and adolescent responses

**Source:** Sherman, L. E., et al. (2016). *The Power of the Like in Adolescence: Effects of Peer Influence on Neural and Behavioral Responses to Social Media.* Psychological Science. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27247125/) · [Author-lab paper](https://greenfieldlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/168/2025/04/sherman-et-al-2016-the-power-of-the-like-in-adolescence-effects-of-peer-influence-on-neural-and-behavioral-responses-to.pdf) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797616645673).

**Finding and scope:** popularity information influenced endorsement and reward-related fMRI responses in adolescents using a simulated feed. It does not measure dopamine depletion, establish lasting injury, or describe every adult.

**Application:** [Unit 2](../courses/embodied-digital-life/02-attention-and-the-urge-to-check.md) separates social influence from an unsupported chemical-damage story.

## E3. Evening light and sleep

**Source:** Chang, A.-M., Aeschbach, D., Duffy, J. F., & Czeisler, C. A. (2015). *Evening use of light-emitting eReaders negatively affects sleep, circadian timing, and next-morning alertness.* Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25535358/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418490112).

**Finding and scope:** a small controlled crossover comparison of prolonged evening electronic versus print reading found differences in circadian timing, melatonin, sleep onset, and morning alertness. Its conditions do not predict every everyday exposure.

**Application:** [Unit 5](../courses/embodied-digital-life/05-transitions-into-rest.md) distinguishes light, task duration, and unfinished content. Supported rest was not tested as an antidote.

## E4. Changing smartphone use

**Source:** Pieh, C., et al. (2025). *Smartphone screen time reduction improves mental health: a randomized controlled trial.* BMC Medicine, 23, 107. [Full paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-025-03944-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39985031/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-025-03944-z).

**Finding and scope:** a three-week reduction intervention in healthy young adult students produced small-to-medium changes in several self-reported outcomes. The trial was unblinded and use later rebounded. It did not establish a universal safe duration or brain repair.

**Application:** [Unit 1](../courses/embodied-digital-life/01-notice-your-digital-pattern.md) introduces careful testing of habits, without prescribing the trial's limit to learners.

## E5. Brief structured breathing

**Source:** Balban, M. Y., et al. (2023). *Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal.* Cell Reports Medicine, 4, 100895. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895).

**Finding and scope:** a small remote randomized comparison found advantages for positive mood and respiratory rate, particularly with exhale-focused cyclic sighing. Resting heart rate and heart-rate variability did not significantly change; the study did not establish a sleep benefit.

**Application:** [Unit 3](../courses/embodied-digital-life/03-emotional-momentum-and-breath.md) compares findings rather than reproducing the techniques. The course's ordinary unpaced breath-awareness invitation differs from the structured breathing interventions and is not promised their effects.

## E6. A matched breathing comparison

**Source:** Fincham, G. W., Strauss, C., & Cavanagh, K. (2023). *Effect of coherent breathing on mental health and wellbeing: a randomised placebo-controlled trial.* Scientific Reports, 13, 22141. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38092805/) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10719279/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-49279-8).

**Finding and scope:** in a blinded adult trial, slow coherent breathing did not outperform a matched paced-breathing comparison on the main mental-health outcomes. This challenges claims for a uniquely effective pace; it does not test all possible breathing practices.

**Application:** [Unit 3](../courses/embodied-digital-life/03-emotional-momentum-and-breath.md) keeps this result alongside E5. Neither source justifies prescribing a universal ratio or nervous-system reset.

## E7. Interrupting prolonged sitting

**Source:** Benzo, R. M., et al. (2018). *Acute Effects of Interrupting Sitting on Discomfort and Alertness of Office Workers.* Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine, 60, 804–809. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29608539/) · [Full text at CDC](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/212254/cdc_212254_DS1.pdf) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/JOM.0000000000001329).

**Finding and scope:** a small randomized crossover study of selected inactive office workers with overweight compared sitting with hourly standing or pedaling interruptions. Recruitment excluded some health conditions and orthopedic limitations. Reported discomfort was lower with interruptions; alertness effects were short-lived. It did not test this course's seated movements or establish treatment for a pain condition.

**Application:** [Unit 4](../courses/embodied-digital-life/04-movement-and-visual-comfort.md) explores session design, optional movement, and retained access. It does not claim that planning-only alternatives reproduce movement's physical effects.

## E8. Visual comfort guidance

**Sources:** American Academy of Ophthalmology, [Computers, Digital Devices, and Eye Strain](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/computer-usage) and [Should You Be Worried About Blue Light?](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/should-you-be-worried-about-blue-light).

**Guidance and scope:** regular breaks, blinking, and suitable viewing conditions are practical recommendations for digital eye discomfort. The 20-20-20 rule is guidance rather than an exact experimentally established optimum. Visual discomfort and the circadian effects of evening light are different questions.

**Application:** [Unit 4](../courses/embodied-digital-life/04-movement-and-visual-comfort.md) offers optional visual choices and nonvisual learning alternatives. It does not prescribe eye exercises, guarantee prevention, or claim that a sensory pause treats an eye condition.

## E9. Yoga evidence and variation

**Source:** National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, [Yoga: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety).

**Summary and scope:** research suggests benefits for some wellness and symptom outcomes, with substantial variation in yoga programs and study quality. Low-back-pain benefits over no exercise are generally small; differences from other exercise are uncertain. This summary does not establish technology-specific neural repair.

### Yoga and the scope of the course

Yoga is rooted in Indian philosophy and includes diverse traditions of practice. The Commons acknowledges that context rather than equating yoga with a few movements. The original gentle postural invitations here support an educational exploration of participation. They are not a full yoga course, a clinical protocol, or evidence that a specific posture counteracts a specific neural change. No Ahava Way certification sequence is reproduced.

## E10. Social-media context and individual differences

**Source:** National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2024). *Social Media and Adolescent Health.* [Summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/27396/chapter/2) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.17226/27396).

**Summary and scope:** the report describes potential benefits and harms, design incentives, and variation among young people. Its review did not establish a population-wide causal effect on adolescent health. This is not proof that no individual can be harmed, and adult findings should not be substituted for youth evidence.

**Application:** educators should preserve useful connection and access while addressing particular problematic conditions. Total time alone does not describe the whole interaction.

## Claims and proposed practices

| Statement | Treatment in the course |
| --- | --- |
| A notification can interfere with a demanding task in studied conditions. | Supported by E1 within its experimental scope. |
| Social feedback can influence adolescent endorsement and reward-related activity. | Supported by E2; do not substitute a direct dopamine measurement. |
| Some defined digital-use changes can affect measured outcomes. | Supported for the particular interventions and populations; do not infer universal limits. |
| This original three-minute pause will regulate physiology, improve judgment, or repair harm. | Not established. Offer it as a choice without an effectiveness claim. |
| A small seated movement has the same effect as studied standing or pedaling. | Not established. Do not equate different activities. |
| Feeling calmer makes an AI claim more trustworthy. | Invalid inference. Check the claim's evidence independently. |
| A personal experiment with a useful result proves a medical or neural change. | Not established. It can inform a practical individual choice. |

## Review and maintenance

Before revising a teaching claim, check the original source, its population, measured outcome, comparator, corrections, and relevance to the proposed activity. Record why the claim changed. Include neutral findings and meaningful counterexamples, not only studies that fit a preferred story.

Priorities for review are practitioner scrutiny of invitations, accessibility feedback from learners, and usability of the lesson sequence. Participation must not become a test of compliance. Collect feedback on instructions and access without health histories, private logs, or identifiable learner data. A later formal research project requires its own appropriate ethics, consent, and study design; participation in this course does not authorize one.
