# Research, reading, and evidence boundaries

[Home](../README.md)

The initial practices have not been evaluated for effectiveness. They offer opportunities to observe and reflect; they do not claim nervous-system regulation, clinical benefit, enhanced decision accuracy, or guaranteed attention restoration. “I found this useful” is a valid experience report, not a general causal conclusion.

## Distinctions for reading

- **Observation:** what a participant reports or what a defined measure records.
- **Interpretation:** an explanation of that observation; alternatives may remain.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposal, such as whether a transition helps learners identify the next task.
- **Practice:** an activity offered here; not automatically a tested intervention.
- **Normative framework:** the Commons' commitments to consent, accessibility, and non-domination.
- **Open question:** whether, for whom, and under what conditions an activity helps or creates burdens.

The broad embodied premise does not validate every proposed embodiment practice. Likewise, research on one breathing or movement intervention cannot automatically support these different, self-selected activities.

## Reading directions

Begin with primary studies or scholarly reviews on embodied cognition, interoception, attention, environmental conditions, and accessibility. Before importing a claim, record the exact intervention, comparison, population, outcome measure, limitations, and whether it matches the activity here. No particular clinical protocol or physiological theory is prescribed by this release.

For the educational context of AI, [UNESCO's 2023 guidance](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research) offers a policy perspective on human agency and inclusion. It is not evidence for the effectiveness of these practices.

## Practical v0.2 inquiry

First collect voluntary usability and accessibility feedback: Were instructions clear? Were alternatives meaningful? Was declining easy? Avoid collecting health histories or treating workshop participation as consent to research. Any formal human-participant study requires an appropriate ethics and consent process for its setting.

A later learning study could compare an optional transition with an ordinary break, predefine a modest outcome such as task clarity, and report neutral or adverse experiences as well as benefits. Do not infer a biological mechanism from subjective reports alone.
