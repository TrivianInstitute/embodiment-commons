# Classroom inquiry — Attention and place

[Home](../README.md) · 20 minutes · Adaptable for adults or educator-supported learners

**Learning goal:** distinguish a personal observation from a general claim and identify an environmental factor in participation. No AI account or bodily disclosure is needed.

1. In 3 minutes, describe the goal and alternatives. Learners may analyze the fictional example below instead of noticing their own experience.
2. For 4 minutes, try one comfortable external-attention practice or take a quiet break. Keep assistive technology available.
3. In 5 minutes, record three columns: observation; possible interpretation; change to try. An example is “I lost my place when the corridor became loud; sound may be affecting my reading; try another seat if available.”
4. For 5 minutes, compare environmental changes without identifying classmates. Consider whether the proposed change helps everyone or creates a new barrier.
5. In 3 minutes, complete an exit response: one observation does not establish __; a question I could investigate is __.

**Fictional alternative:** A learner concentrates better near a window one day and concludes natural light always improves learning. What else may differ—noise, time, task, seating, expectation? How could they investigate without turning classmates into involuntary study subjects?

**Assessment:** look for separation of observation and interpretation, attention to alternatives, and a practical change. Do not assess bodily stillness, calm, or willingness to speak. No causal conclusion is warranted from this activity.
