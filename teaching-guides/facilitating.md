# Facilitating an optional practice

[Home](../README.md)

**Before:** read the chosen practice and its cautions. Explain its purpose in everyday language, its duration, and its alternatives. Check that people can remain seated, move comfortably, keep assistive devices active, or decline. Do not ask for health histories. These are educational invitations, not clinical screening or treatment.

**Invitation script:** “We have a few minutes to notice how we are arriving. You can follow the practice, choose an external point of attention, write quietly, or take an ordinary break. You can stop at any time. Nothing needs to be shared.”

**During:** use neutral language such as “you may notice” rather than “you should feel.” Leave enough time for a person to choose. No touch, posture correction, closed-eye requirement, breath pacing, or public evaluation. If someone stops, let them stop without questioning. If a participant reports discomfort, end the activity for them and offer space or a practical alternative; do not interpret the cause.

**After:** invite a private or voluntary reflection: useful, neutral, uncomfortable, or not tried. Ask about the instructions and access conditions rather than a disclosure of personal history. Do not turn one person's response into evidence about everyone. Do not promise a physiological explanation.

**A 15-minute teaching format:** 3 minutes of invitation and options; 3 minutes of practice or alternative; 4 minutes of private reflection; 5 minutes discussing how a setting supports attention. The intended learning is to distinguish observation, interpretation, and environmental choice, not to achieve calm.

**Feedback:** ask whether the directions were understandable, whether an equivalent alternative existed, and what should change. Participation rate, quietness, or reported relaxation is not a measure of consent, therapeutic benefit, or learning.
