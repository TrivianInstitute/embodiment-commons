## Embodied and situated learning

People learn with bodies, histories, relationships, and environments. Here that premise motivates questions about posture options, sensory conditions, fatigue, access, attention, and participation. It does not establish a single theory of cognition or show that a particular movement produces a particular psychological effect.

The Commons treats bodily experience as information someone may choose to consider. Sensation and interpretation are distinct. A tight shoulder does not identify the cause of a disagreement; a feeling of ease does not establish that a decision is just. Evidence, values, authority, and lived experience each require their own examination.

## Consent and non-domination

These are normative commitments of Trivian education. A participant may observe, adapt, pause, or decline. Facilitators do not touch, reposition, diagnose, or interpret participants. They do not equate eye contact, stillness, camera use, or breath rhythm with attention or consent. A practice becomes less educational when compliance with it becomes a condition of belonging.

## Nervous-system literacy without overclaiming

“Nervous system” names a biological system, not a label a facilitator can reliably assign from someone's appearance. In this release, literacy means recognizing the limits of inference and avoiding unsupported explanations such as claiming that a short exercise resets a person's nervous system. Learners may describe their own sensations without being required to map them onto a physiological theory.

Interoception refers broadly to sensing internal bodily conditions; noticing an internal sensation does not ensure correct interpretation of its cause. Sensory awareness may concern external conditions. Both can be explored, and neither is required for participation. This release offers no autonomic-state assessment or clinical intervention.

## Research status

The practices are original educational invitations, not tested protocols. Claims that they improve performance, regulate physiology, relieve a condition, or restore attention would require evidence appropriate to those specific claims. The absence of a benefit in someone's experience is not a failure of effort.

## Boundaries

No forced breathing, breath retention, hands-on stretch, treatment, diagnosis, or credentialing is included. Paid Ahava Way certification content is excluded. Keep an ordinary break available; sometimes the needed change is workload, accessibility, or an institutional decision rather than an individual practice.
