# Workplace transitions without compulsory wellness

[Home](../README.md)

A team can make room for attention without requiring employees to perform a bodily practice. Offer two or three minutes between cognitively different tasks, and let people choose movement, quiet, an accessible sensory shift, or their own transition. Keep the option separate from performance evaluation.

**A meeting transition:** save the current decision; identify what remains unresolved; offer a brief break; reopen by naming the next decision and who has authority. A facilitator may link to the movement transition or collaboration check-in, but should not track individual completion or infer wellbeing from participation.

**A design question:** if people repeatedly need recovery because meetings run without breaks, change the schedule. If a workspace is inaccessible, address that condition. Do not present an individual attention practice as the solution to excessive workload, discrimination, noise, or lack of decision power.

**Team reflection:** Did the transition make the next task clearer? Was declining easy? Did anyone lack an equivalent option? Discuss process and access, not colleagues' bodies or private circumstances. Feedback can be anonymous or private; a manager should not solicit health disclosures.

These practices provide no clinical or productivity guarantee. Evaluate the actual work conditions and avoid turning voluntary participation into an implicit requirement.
