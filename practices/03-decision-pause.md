# An embodied pause before consequential decisions

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Notice urgency or bodily cues while keeping evidence, values, and authority distinct. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 2–5 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** Before a non-urgent choice affecting yourself or others.

## Instructions

1. Write the decision in one sentence. Check whether a short pause is practical; skip it when immediate action is needed.

2. Choose an anchor: ordinary unaltered breathing, contact with a support, or an external object. Do not slow, deepen, or hold your breath deliberately.

3. If comfortable, notice whether you register tension, ease, tiredness, restlessness, or no clear sensation. These are observations, not a verdict.

4. Name one interpretation you are making about that observation and one alternative. “I feel tense” does not establish “this choice is wrong.”

5. Return to the decision: What evidence matters? Who is affected? Who may authorize the action? What is the cost of waiting?

6. Choose to proceed, seek information, consult an authorized person, or defer. Write the reason separately from how your body feels.

## Accessibility and modifications

Breath attention is optional. Use an external anchor or simply answer the decision questions. Respond verbally, in writing, or with a partner who records your words without interpreting them. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

Did you confuse urgency with importance, or comfort with correctness? What fact could still change your decision? A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

Bodily sensations do not prove truth, safety, deception, or another person's intent. Do not use this practice to bypass evidence or professional advice. Stop inward attention if it increases discomfort. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care.
