# Noticing bodily cues during uncertainty

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Separate sensation, interpretation, and decision without making bodily feeling an oracle. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 4–6 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** A low-stakes reflection on an unresolved question.

## Instructions

1. Choose a low-stakes unresolved question, such as which format to try for a study session. Do not select a crisis or distressing personal event.

2. If comfortable, notice one bodily sensation while considering the question. If not, notice an external cue or write “no sensation selected.”

3. Make three short entries: observation; interpretation; information still needed. Example: “Hands restless; I might be impatient; I do not yet know the time available.”

4. Generate a second possible interpretation. Restlessness could have many meanings; the exercise does not identify its cause.

5. Choose one small next inquiry or reversible experiment. Keep the observation distinct from the reason for your choice.

## Accessibility and modifications

Internal attention is optional and some people may not identify a clear sensation. Use a fictional example, an external observation, or the information-needed column alone. No public sharing is required. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

Where did you move from noticing to explaining? What prevented an interpretation from becoming an unsupported certainty? A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

This does not diagnose nervous-system states or teach lie detection. Do not ask a group to relive difficult events. End the activity if it is uncomfortable; no completion is required. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care. For a group, see [teaching guidance](../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) before offering the practice.
