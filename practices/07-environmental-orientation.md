# Orienting to place after digital focus

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Reconnect attention with the local environment and notice how it supports or constrains participation. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 3–7 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** After prolonged digital work or before entering a shared space.

## Instructions

1. Save your place and choose a safe location from which to observe. You can remain seated and keep assistive devices active.

2. Notice one feature of the environment through an available sense: light, sound, contact, airflow, spatial arrangement, or another comfortable channel.

3. Notice a practical relationship: where people pass, what is within reach, where privacy is limited, or what makes participation easier. Do not observe or record others invasively.

4. Identify one small adjustment you can make or request, such as moving an object, changing a sound level, or choosing a different place. Leaving things unchanged is an option.

5. Before returning, ask what the next activity requires from this place and from the people sharing it.

## Accessibility and modifications

No outdoor access, walking, vision, or quiet room is required. A mental map, sound pattern, or familiar object can serve. If the environment is overwhelming, choose one neutral feature or skip observation. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

What environmental condition had you treated as fixed? Who might experience this setting differently? A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

This is not a guarantee of restoration or safety. Do not practice while driving, crossing traffic, or doing tasks that require uninterrupted attention. Do not change shared access arrangements without consulting affected people. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care. For a group, see [teaching guidance](../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) before offering the practice.
