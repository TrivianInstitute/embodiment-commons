# A movement transition between cognitive tasks

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Mark a transition with a self-selected change in position, movement, or attention. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 2–4 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** Between meetings, lessons, or focused tasks.

## Instructions

1. Finish or mark the current task so it can be resumed. Look for a clear, stable space if you plan to move.

2. Choose one familiar, comfortable movement or position change: adjust your seated position, move your hands, stand if appropriate, or take a short familiar route. Stillness with a change in attention is equally valid.

3. Move at a comfortable pace within a familiar range. There is no stretch target, repetition count, or required intensity.

4. Pause or stop whenever you choose. Notice whether you want to keep the new position or return.

5. Name the next task and one concrete starting action before re-entering it.

## Accessibility and modifications

Adapt to mobility aids, fatigue, pain, available space, and individual preferences. Imagining the transition or moving attention to a different object is an alternative. No one should assist, touch, or reposition another person. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

Did the transition help you distinguish the tasks? Which version would you choose next time? A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

Avoid painful or unstable movements. Stop if discomfort, dizziness, or other concerning sensations arise. This is not a mobility treatment, exercise prescription, assisted stretch, or certification technique. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care. For a group, see [teaching guidance](../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) before offering the practice.
