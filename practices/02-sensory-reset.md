# A short post-screen sensory reset

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Explore a change in sensory attention and notice the surrounding environment. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 2–5 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** After digital work, reading, or concentrated near-focus activity.

## Instructions

1. Pause the activity at a recoverable point. Save your place if needed.

2. Choose one comfortable external channel: looking around, listening, feeling a supported contact, or noticing temperature. You need not use every sense.

3. Attend to one nearby feature and, if available and comfortable, one farther-away feature. Describe each neutrally without searching for a special feeling.

4. Let your attention move at its own pace for a minute. You may shift position, remain still, or leave the practice.

5. Choose the next transition: return, take a longer break, change location, or stop for now.

## Accessibility and modifications

For visual sensitivity or blindness, use sound or contact. For sound sensitivity or hearing differences, use another available channel. Keep assistive technology available; “screen-free” is not a requirement. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

What did you notice that had dropped out of attention? Did the change feel useful, neutral, or uncomfortable? A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

This is not an eye-treatment protocol or a promise of attention restoration. Avoid bright light, uncomfortable sound, or any sensory input you know is difficult for you. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care. For a group, see [teaching guidance](../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) before offering the practice.
