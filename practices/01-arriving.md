# Arriving before an AI interaction

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Make the transition into an interaction deliberate and name what participation means for this task. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 2–4 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** Before a writing, planning, or learning exchange.

## Instructions

1. Leave the interface unopened for a moment, or stop typing. Choose a position that is comfortable enough; no ideal posture is required.

2. Notice one available point of contact or one feature of the room. If bodily attention is unwelcome, simply name the task in writing.

3. Ask: What am I here to do? What judgment do I intend to retain? What information should stay out of this exchange?

4. Write one sentence of purpose and one boundary. For example: “Explore three event ideas; do not send invitations or include attendee information.”

5. Begin, or choose to defer. Set a review point appropriate to the task, such as after the first outline.

## Accessibility and modifications

Use speech, an accessible note, or a mental sentence. No vision, standing, stillness, or internal sensation is required. Someone who benefits from movement may continue moving comfortably. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

Compare your first request with the purpose you named. Did the brief arrival reveal a question or boundary? No change is also a valid observation. A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

Do not delay an urgent necessary action to perform the practice. This does not secure data or establish technical permissions; those need actual settings and agreements. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care. For a group, see [teaching guidance](../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) before offering the practice.
