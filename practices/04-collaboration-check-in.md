# Checking in during extended collaboration

[Practice index](README.md) · Status: v0.1, unpiloted educational practice

## Purpose

Notice the conditions of participation and renegotiate the next part of the activity. This is an invitation to explore attention, not a clinical intervention or an established treatment.

**Duration:** 3–5 minutes. Shorten or stop at any point.

**Context:** During a long AI conversation, study group, writing session, or meeting.

## Instructions

1. At a natural stopping point, briefly summarize what has been accomplished and what remains.

2. Choose whether to notice your surroundings, your posture or contact, or only the task itself. Ask whether continuing in the same way is workable.

3. Identify a practical need if one is apparent: a break, food or water when appropriate, less noise, a different format, clarification, or no change. You need not disclose why.

4. Ask: Am I still examining the proposals, or only approving the next response? Name one decision that needs independent attention.

5. Change one condition or explicitly choose to continue. In a group, agree when to review again without requiring everyone to have the same need.

## Accessibility and modifications

Offer a private response, a pass, or a simple “continue/change/break” choice. People may need to keep moving, remain seated, or avoid bodily attention. Do not require cameras. Opting out is a complete participation choice. No explanation or personal disclosure is required.

## Reflection

What changed in the process, if anything? Did the check-in create space for dissent or become another demand? A neutral result or decision not to continue is useful feedback, not failure.

## Relevant cautions

Facilitators must not infer fatigue, regulation, consent, or engagement from posture, gaze, or stillness. A check-in is not permission to inspect another person's body or private circumstances. These materials do not replace medical or mental-health care. For a group, see [teaching guidance](../teaching-guides/facilitating.md) before offering the practice.
